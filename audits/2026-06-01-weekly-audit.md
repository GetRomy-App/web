## 2. Patterns across the week — the writer has converged on a template

This is the single biggest thing the founder needs to see. Every post this week was built from the same eight beats, in the same order:

1. Single-name character, doing one small specific action, with weather/time/location.
2. **"We've been thinking about [X] a lot lately."** — appears in 6/6 posts.
3. A second-level header that begins **"The thing/number/signal/guilt nobody [reads/wants to look at/questions]."**
4. **"Here's the thing nobody tells you."** — appears in 5/6 posts as a header.
5. **"Why we miss her (and why it isn't your fault)."** — appears in 5/6 posts *as a header*.
6. **"What we want from a tool" / "What we want a tool to do here"** containing the sentence pattern: *"Rōmy doesn't [verb]. A person does that — has to. … That part stays yours. It always was."* — 6/6.
7. **"The boring revolution, again"** beginning **"We keep landing in the same unglamorous place."** — 6/6.
8. **"A small assignment, with love ♡"** closing with **"Go be the person who…"** — 5/6.

Every individual sentence in this scaffold is good. Six in a row in a week is no longer a voice — it's a content-management workflow. A reader who reads any two posts back-to-back will feel the template before they feel the story. That's how a brand voice ages out of being a worldview and into being a verse-chorus-verse.

**Concrete repeat-phrase audit** (verbatim recurrences):
- *"a person does that — has to"* — 6/6
- *"That part stays yours. It always was."* — 6/6
- *"We keep landing in the same unglamorous place"* — 6/6
- *"every claim hyperlinked to where it came from"* / *"linked to where it came from"* — 5/6
- *"from the public record"* — 6/6, often twice per post
- *"the future of fundraising isn't [X]. It's a development officer who…"* — 6/6
- *"with love ♡"* sign-off — 5/6
- *"the kind of [X]"* / *"the only [register/measure/question] [that/X] [verb]"* — every post has at least one
- Opening name-on-a-Tuesday-in-March construction — 4/6 explicitly anchor in a Tuesday

**Other drift signals:**
- The character names are starting to leak across posts. **Priya** is the founder in 05-31 and the development officer in 05-26. **Margaret** is the donor's mother in 05-30 and Mrs. Halloran in the older 05-07 post. The blog reads serial; the names need to.
- The "Rōmy product paragraph" is now in a fixed slot — third-to-last section, opens with "Rōmy doesn't [X]," ends with "every claim linked to where it came from." Six identical product placements in a week reads like a sponsor break.
- Every CTA is a Tuesday-or-Wednesday small homework with a flicker of recognition at the end. Same emotional beat six times.

We are not yet drifting toward feature-listing — the product is still framed humbly. We **are** drifting toward incantation. The voice is becoming a chant.

## 3. The week's best post — and why

**The Tuesday the Money Landed (05-31).** It worked because it picks the biggest fight: the entire prospect-research industry is built for the wrong tense. *"Wealth screens are obituary work."* That is the kind of line a Red Bull CMO would put on a wall. The post takes a worldview ("scan what is") and replaces it with a different worldview ("scan what just happened"), and it does so with a fully built character (Priya in the parking lot) that costs the reader no effort to remember tomorrow.

It is also the most *exportable* post of the week — three or four lines from it can survive as standalone tweets, posters, or talking points. That's the bar. Mirror this one. Specifically, mirror its **structural courage**: it doesn't run the "Why we miss her (and why it isn't your fault)" template header. It opens new ground inside the formula.

## 4. The week's worst post — and what it should have been

**The Introduction Is the Gift (05-26).** Not because the prose is bad — it isn't — but because the *stance* is the conventional wisdom of the trade. Every fundraiser already believes warm introductions beat cold prospecting. We told them what they already know, in beautiful sentences, and got out. A Red Bull CMO would say: if the audience nods on the headline, we lost.

**What it should have been:** a post that argues the opposite of what fundraisers already feel — for instance, *"Your best donors don't want to introduce you. They want to keep you."* The Walt-at-table-six character could have been used to show that the most loyal donors *guard* their friends from being asked, and the work is to be the rare nonprofit they'd trust to make the call.

**Suggested rewritten hook:**

> Walt has stacked the chairs at this gala eleven years running. He has never once introduced his old college roommate to the development director. Not because he hasn't thought about it. Because he has.
>
> Your most loyal donor is a quiet wall around the people he loves. The job isn't to ask him for the name. The job is to become the rare organization he'd trust to be told it.

That's the same character, the same scene, an actual fight.

## 5. Three angles to try next week — each a stance, not a topic

1. **"Capacity scores are the astrology of fundraising."** — A frontal attack on the wealth-screen industry as pseudoscience: confident, charts-laden, and predicts almost nothing about who actually gives. Sets up the "who just got money" worldview from 05-31 as a *replacement*, not an addendum.

2. **"The gala is the worst place to meet a donor."** — Argues that the most expensive convening on the fundraising calendar is the one where you are guaranteed not to have the conversation that matters. The room everyone has been told *is* the room is the wrong room. Builds a worldview where the work is everywhere except the marquee event.

3. **"Most boards are alumni of indifference."** — A defendable, mildly dangerous stance: boards are populated by people who once cared, are now scheduled, and serve as a structural alibi for the development office. Pivots into what a board could be if it were treated as the most underused asset on the file rather than a quarterly meeting.

(Each of these is a stance the reader can disagree with. That's the bar — the post earns its read by being arguable.)

## 6. Brand voice drift watch — retire list

The daily writer has earned a vocabulary. It is now leaning on it. Retire the following for at least two weeks; let them air out.

- **"We've been thinking about [X] a lot lately."** — Used to be a soft pivot. Now it's a content-template signal. Used 6/6.
- **"Here's the thing nobody tells you."** — Was once a promise; is now a tic.
- **The "Why we miss her (and why it isn't your fault)" header.** — 5/6 posts in a week. It's now a chapter title in a book we didn't realize we were writing.
- **"The boring revolution, again."** — This was a great concept *once*. Six "agains" in a week makes the concept itself feel boring. Either retire it for a month or commit to it as a recurring series and brand it deliberately (e.g., a labeled column).
- **"We keep landing in the same unglamorous place."** — Verbatim 6/6. Stop.
- **"a person does that — has to … that part stays yours. It always was."** — Beautiful sentence. The first time. Six times is a jingle. Vary the phrasing of where the human still belongs.
- **"every claim hyperlinked to where it came from / from the public record"** — Product paragraph boilerplate. Pick one product paragraph and use it 1 in 4 posts; the other 3 posts should carry the worldview without selling.
- **"with love ♡"** sign-off + the heart glyph — 5/6. The heart needs to mean something. It currently means *Tuesday.*
- **"Go be the person who…"** closing imperative — 5/6. Replace half of these with closings that *don't* moralize at the reader.
- **Character names leaking across posts** — Priya (used twice this week), Margaret/Mrs. Halloran (used twice across the month). Maintain a names file. The audience will assume continuity unless we cue them otherwise.

### One additional creative recommendation

Skip a day this week. We shipped 6/7. The missing day was not a problem; it was an opportunity. Daily cadence is what created the template. A 5-of-7 cadence with two posts that *deliberately break the formula* would be more valuable than 7-of-7 incantations.
