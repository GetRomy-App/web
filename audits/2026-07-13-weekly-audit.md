## 2. Detailed scoring

**The Ledger Knew in April (07-06)** — Hook **4**, Stance **5**, Story **4**, Thesis **5**, Distribution **4**, CTA **5** → **4.50**

The opener is three words: *"Delia Marchetti opens the mail."* It is the first non-templated opener the blog has shipped in eleven days. It does not tell the reader the color of the shingles, the brand of the pen, or the name of the cat. It does not tell the reader Delia's age, her marital status, whether her husband is on hospice, or how long she has been widowed. It tells the reader one fact, in five stressed monosyllables, that is doing all the work: **a woman opens the mail.** The reader is in the scene at word three. Red Bull's writers hand a reader a surprise in paragraph two; this hook hands her a *character with a job* in paragraph one. That is not a template. That is a *sentence.*

The stance is the strongest architectural claim the blog has made in a month: *"A well-run development operation is not one that hires another prospect researcher. It is one that treats every desk in the building as a listening post."* It generalizes — the receptionist, the events coordinator, the volunteer coordinator, the archivist, the controller are all named — and it is *operational.* The reader can act on it Monday, and the action is not *"send a plain cream card in your own hand"* — it is *"walk downstairs, buy Delia a coffee, ask her what she is seeing."* That is a physical action a working development director can perform this week. It is not a card.

The story is Delia, coding a check on a Wednesday morning at 9:14, walking it to the safe, dropping the envelope in Thursday's bin. She has agency. She is not a set piece. She is not observed by a narrator sitting six feet away. She *does* something — she updates the DNM flag the same Wednesday morning — and the post's central tragedy (development learns six weeks later, at the third bullet of the finance update) is a *system failure* the reader can recognize in her own building. Delia is not a saint. She is a working finance director who did exactly what her job asked her to do. That is the strongest kind of character a marketing post can build: a person who did nothing wrong, and yet.

The thesis is compressible to one line: *"The check landed in April. The ledger knew first."* It is the tweet. It is the T-shirt. It is the sentence the working development director sends her director of development at 10:47 p.m. on a Sunday with a note that says *this.* It is also, structurally, a three-beat closing that lands with the same weight as the strongest closings the blog has shipped in two months. It is not soft. It does not apologize. It does not concede the pitch.

Distribution is high. Section headers (*"Everyone in the building is on the pipeline,"* *"The asymmetry no development plan touches,"* *"The condolence that arrives in the wrong week,"* *"The org chart of a working development office"*) are quotable in isolation. The *"receipt with feelings"* insight from 07-08 is prefigured here: *"A note that reaches Julia in June... is not a condolence. It is a receipt with feelings"* — wait: this line is actually in 07-08, not 07-06. The 07-06 equivalent is *"a note that reaches Julia the second week of April... is opened, cried over, framed on a hallway shelf, and remembered for the rest of Julia's life"* — different rhythm, same claim. Both are quotable.

The Rōmy graf is the best in two months. It does not open with *"Rōmy does not X."* It opens with *"Rōmy sits inside that wiring"* — a positive, spatial, mechanical description. It then describes a specific product mechanism: *"When Delia codes an inbound check Estate, Rōmy pings the development director the same afternoon — with the donor's file already surfaced, the sister's name pulled from the ACH history, the last board year cross-referenced, a draft condolence letter waiting, and a note flagging that the sister lives ninety minutes from the office and drove up for the fall gala in 2022."* This is what the product does. It is the honest thing. No self-effacement. No *"Rōmy does not sit on the dock."* No *"That work is Priscilla's."* No *"Rōmy does not write the card."*

The CTA is the closing three-beat itself. There is no *"A small assignment, with love ♡"* header. There is no *"Turn to it. ♡"* sign-off. The post closes with *"Most development directors do not know it yet. They are on the second floor. They are running a plan. / The check landed in April. The ledger knew first."* That IS the CTA. The stance is the ask. If the reader takes the sentence to heart, the action follows — she walks downstairs on Monday. Red Bull does not close its ads with *"a small assignment, with love ♡."* It closes with *"gives you wings."* This closes with *"the ledger knew first."* They are the same *shape.*

Costs: the post has one archetype the writer should audit — the *"woman in a small back office quietly doing the org's most important work while the second floor runs a plan"* archetype. It appeared in 07-05 (Rowan Vasquez by the copier) and 07-06 (Delia Marchetti at the ledger). It is the strongest new archetype the blog has built in a month. It is also, at two consecutive posts, in danger of becoming *its own liturgy.* The audit's second recommendation, below, will name three ways to extend it without templating it.

**Zero ♡. Zero *"A small assignment, with love"* header. Zero widow. Zero cat. Zero shingled cape. Zero brand-name pen. Zero Nan Fogarty.** The 07-06 post is what the writer produces when she is not writing to a template. It is what she is capable of on any Monday. On seven of the next eight Mondays she has instead produced something else.

**The Note That Wasn't Sent (07-08)** — Hook **3**, Stance **5**, Story **5**, Thesis **5**, Distribution **4**, CTA **3** → **4.17**

The opener is templated but the character has *agency* by paragraph three. Meg Hilbert opens the *Greenfield Recorder* — a real Massachusetts paper — to a real page (B4) at a real time (7:42 a.m.), on a Tuesday morning she has kept as a habit for years. She is not a set piece. She is a working development director in a second-floor office over a bookstore, and by nine she is going to try to write a card. The reader is in the scene, and the reader is in Meg's *plan.*

The stance is the sharpest single-line insight of the week: *"It is not a condolence. It is a receipt with feelings."* Followed by *"The card is still there."* Followed by *"Grief is not a moment. It is a sorting."* This is the writer at full voice. This is what she is capable of when she is not describing the color of the shingles.

The story has a full three-act structure: Meg reads the obit at 7:42, means to write the card at 9:00, is derailed by the CFO's 8:58 phone call, the budget meeting, the interim provost, the foundation call, the corporate-partner coffee. At 5:15 p.m. on Friday she opens the drawer, takes out the Crane's card, uncaps the Uni-ball, sits for a moment, and *puts the pen down.* She thinks — *in a voice she will not be proud of* — *she will get a lot of notes this week. Mine will be one more.* This is a *decision.* A wrong one. It is the most honest scene in a Rōmy post since *The Advisor in Her Town.* The reader has been in that room. The reader has, on a Friday at 5:15, decided that hers would be one more. The post *knows* her.

The eleven-months coda — Louisa Kittredge moving the college's brochure from the counter to the drawer with the takeout menus — is the second-strongest single beat. It is Nan-Fogarty-understands-grief-is-a-sorting made physical, in a specific woman's kitchen, with a specific brochure Peter had circled in blue Bic. It is what the templated form is supposed to do, and rarely does.

Distribution: Section breaks work. The paragraphs are dense but the beats land in short sentences (*"She reads it twice."* / *"Louisa Kittredge, class of 1961, has been giving to the college every December since 1976."* / *"She does not close the Recorder."* / *"It is Friday. The service was on Wednesday."* / *"She closes the drawer. The card, unsent, will sit there for eleven months."*). These are quotable.

CTA: *"A small assignment, with love ♡"* header + *"Turn to it. ♡"* sign-off. The instruction — read the local paper, write three sentences, walk to the post office, hand it across to Rick — is specific and physical. But the CTA closes with the templated shape, and the *"Turn to it. ♡"* is now the writer's default cadence. Score 3, held down by the liturgy.

Nan Fogarty makes her debut. She is introduced as a real character — a former director who told Meg over lunch at the People's Pint in the spring of 2019 that the two pages you cannot miss are the death notices and the church calendar. She is credible here. She is the *predecessor.* Then, from 07-09 forward, she becomes the *saint.* This post is where the canonization began, and the audit's concern is not with this post — Nan is fine here — but with what happens next.

> Ruth Halloran, class agent for Wellesley 1958 from 1985 to 2010, moved out of Grafton, Vermont on a Wednesday.

## 7. Three angles to try next week

Each of these is a defendable stance, not a topic. Each avoids widows, cream cards, and small towns in Vermont.

**Angle 1 — "Every desk in the building is a fundraising desk."**

The 07-06 post named finance as a listening post. Do the other five. **The receptionist** knows who came in unannounced last Thursday with a manila folder. **The events coordinator** knows who left the gala at eight-fifteen and who stayed to help fold the linens. **The volunteer coordinator** knows which retired accountant drives fifty minutes on a Saturday to close the boxes. **The archivist** knows whose parents' names are on the wall of the old auditorium. **The controller** knows when an estate closes. Five posts. Five modern workplaces. Five characters with real jobs and real agency. Set them in Louisville, Charlotte, San Antonio, Portland Oregon, Nashville. Zero widows. Zero shingled capes. Zero cats. Zero Nan Fogarty. **Prove the 07-06 post was not an accident.**

Sample opening line: *"The best major-gift officer on the payroll last quarter was the receptionist. She has never asked for a raise."*

**Angle 2 — "The donor-advised fund is the exit door."**

Fidelity Charitable, Schwab Charitable, and Vanguard Charitable are, quietly, the three largest 501(c)(3)s in the United States. Every time a longtime individual donor moves her giving to a DAF, the receiving nonprofit gets an envelope from a Boston or Malvern PA return address, on a check with no donor's name on it — just *"grant recommendation."* The nonprofit thanks the DAF sponsor. The donor never learns whether her gift was received. The relationship, thirty-nine years in, has just been laundered by a Delaware trust. **A working development office can read the DAF envelopes as a leading indicator of a donor moving to full DAF distribution — and can send, before December, a plain honest note that says *"we noticed. Thank you. And thank you for staying with us."*** No ♡. No small assignment. A stance about the intermediation of the estate.

Sample opening line: *"The envelope came in a Malvern, Pennsylvania return address. It did not have Joan Linwood's name on it. Joan gave the college $47,000 in November. The college thanked Vanguard."*

**Angle 3 — "The board is the smallest pipeline in the room."**

The most-repeated line in every gala keynote is that fundraising is done by the board. CASE benchmarks put board-solicited gifts at 12–20% of top-100 major gifts at surveyed institutions. The rest are found, cultivated, and closed by professional staff — often against the board's advice on which donors are "ready." Take the stance. Defend it. Argue with a specific board chair archetype the reader has met: the retired investment banker who wants his portfolio-manager buddy solicited for the naming gift, who has no idea that his buddy's wife is on hospice, who cannot stop naming his own initials in every conversation. **This is the post a working development director will send to her director of development at 9:14 p.m. on a Sunday with a subject line that reads *finally.*** Red Bull's brand does not apologize to its audience. Rōmy's brand does not apologize to boards.

Sample opening line: *"The chair of your board thinks she is your best fundraiser. She is your fourth-best."*
