## 2. Patterns across the week

We have a formula. The formula is so reliably good that we have stopped noticing we are running it.

The week's nine posts follow the same eight-beat structure, in order:

1. Scene with a named character (donor or fundraiser), a specific time, a specific object (yellow check, Subaru, wren card, third coffee cup, Marcus's wave, Christmas card box).
2. One-line pivot: *"We've been thinking about [Name] a lot lately."*
3. Section that names the industry's misdiagnosis ("silent attrition," "the eighty-four-trillion-dollar question," "the sixteen-month job").
4. Aphorism on a line by itself, often italicized or boxed.
5. The contrarian taxonomy: *"X is almost always one of four sentences in disguise."* (Used twice in three days, 05-12 and 05-13.)
6. The "What we want from a tool" section, which is the same disclaimer about Romy: *"Romy's job, in our heads, is not to [do the human part]. The pen is still yours. The [X] is still yours. The remembering is the part we'd like to take."*
7. *"The boring revolution, again"* section, closing with: *"Loud revolutions break things. The boring one [verbs] [name] back."* — appearing in **6 of 9** posts this week with only the verb swapped (gives, lets, notices, writes).
8. *"A small assignment, with love ♡"* — donor-homework prompt ending with "Walk to the post office on Friday. Drop them in the blue box." (verbatim in three posts).

The pattern is the brand and it's also the cage. A reader who reads three posts in a row this week feels expertly soothed. A reader who reads all nine starts to feel the formula. By post seven they can predict the section headers. By post nine they can hum along.

**Specific lines that recur near-verbatim across multiple posts this week:**

- *"The pen is still yours. The visit is still yours. The remembering is the part we'd like to take."* — 05-12, 05-13, 05-15, 05-16, 05-17 (5x in 6 days).
- *"Loud revolutions break things. The boring one [X]."* — 05-08, 05-12, 05-13, 05-14, 05-15, 05-16, 05-17 (7x).
- *"We've been thinking about [Name] a lot lately."* — 05-08, 05-10, 05-12, 05-13, 05-14, 05-15, 05-16, 05-17 (8x).
- *"Walk to the post office on Friday. Drop them in the blue box."* — 05-12, 05-16 (2x verbatim, plus paraphrased in 3 more).
- *"The future of fundraising does not look like a [smarter X / flashier Y]."* — 05-08, 05-13, 05-14, 05-15, 05-16, 05-17 (6x).

These are voice tics now, not signatures.

**One more drift to watch:** the cast has stratified. Every donor is over sixty, recently bereaved, vaguely well-off, and reads like a Marilynne Robinson supporting character. Every honoree is a fourth-grade teacher named something turn-of-the-century (Edna, Eileen, Margaret, Helen, Frank). Every fundraiser is a woman in her twenties with a thrift-store blazer. We have written this character nine times in nine days. She is wonderful. We have noticed. Other characters exist.

## 3. Week's best — and why it worked, in Red Bull terms

**Her First Ask** (2026-05-10).

Red Bull does not sell caffeinated sugar water. Red Bull sells the identity of *the person who would jump out of a balloon at 128,000 feet.* You don't drink the can to perform the act; you drink the can because that's who you already are.

*Her First Ask* is the only post this week that does the same move for our actual buyer.

Every other post in the window is a love letter to the donor. The reader is positioned as the wise, caring fundraiser who is being taught how to notice the donor better. That's nice. It does not sell.

This post inverts. Naomi *is* the reader. The thrift-store blazer is the reader. The 7:22 AM walk to the office is the reader. The bus ride home with the phone in the bag is the reader. The whole post is constructed so that the twenty-four-year-old development associate at a $700K animal shelter reads it and thinks *they see me, they built this for me, I am the protagonist of this profession.*

That is identity sale. That is the worldview. And the closing — *"Tools should narrow the asymmetry. Then they should disappear. Naomi is the future of this field. She always has been. We are quietly building for her."* — is the cleanest brand stance we have published. It is defensible. It picks a fight (with the orthodoxy that assumes a 45-year-old development director). It chooses a side.

Mirror this next week: write to the buyer, not about the donor.

## 4. Week's worst — and the rewrite

**Earn the Second Meeting** (2026-05-11).

What's wrong: it's the only post in the window that opens with "He" and a generic action instead of a specific human in a specific room with a specific object. Mr. Aldana is name-dropped at minute one and abandoned by minute two. The middle of the post is a list of "three sentences that quietly end the meeting." That is a LinkedIn carousel, not a Field Note. The voice survives but the *scene* — the engine of every other post this week — is missing.

What it should have been: the Aldana cameo was the whole post. He looked at his phone twice. He left at 9:47. He never gave. *Why?* That's the post. Not a taxonomy of bad meetings.

**Rewritten hook (drop-in, keep the rest of the voice):**

> Mr. Aldana looked at his phone the second time at minute eleven, and the development director knew. The brochure was already on the table. The case statement was half-out of the bag. At 9:47 he stood up, thanked her warmly, walked east on Lexington, and never gave.
>
> She wrote in the CRM that afternoon: *No interest at this time.* That note was wrong in almost every way a note can be wrong. The interest had been in the room. The brochure had eaten it.

Then run the rest. Don't enumerate "three sentences that quietly end the meeting" — *show* one of them, in Mr. Aldana's actual ear, and let the reader feel the meeting die. The post should leave Aldana, the man, on the page — not "the brochure" as an abstract.

## 5. Three angles to try next week — each a defendable stance

1. **"Your board is the wealth screen you already paid for."**
   Stance: the twelve people who sit across from you four times a year are the most over-screened, under-profiled file in your office. Nobody runs RōmyScore on their own board. We use boards for legitimacy and ignore them as donors. Defend the move; pick the fight with the orthodoxy that treats board-as-governance and board-as-prospect as separate functions.

2. **"Stewardship reports are read by accountants, not donors. Send fewer."**
   Stance: the thirty-page glossy stewardship binder is a compliance artifact for foundations and a guilt offering to individual major donors who never asked for it. The four-sentence handwritten note we already preach beats it on every measurable axis. Pick the fight against the entire stewardship-industrial complex — the design vendors, the impact-report writers, the agencies. *Eight postcards beat one binder.*

3. **"The minor gift is a lie."**
   Stance: there is no such thing as a minor gift, because *every* gift is major to the person who wrote the check. The category exists for the convenience of fundraisers who haven't figured out how to scale attention. Defend a worldview in which the $25 donor is treated as a future $250,000 donor with a slower clock — because the data says she usually is. (This is implicit in *The Eleven-Year Yes.* Make it the explicit pick-a-fight headline post.)

(Bench: "Fundraising consultants are a tax on small shops." "The fall appeal is dying because nobody opens mail — except the people you most need to reach." "Wealth screens are credit checks. CRMs are memory. Stop buying them from the same vendor.")

## 6. Brand voice drift watch — retire these now

Phrases the daily writer is leaning on too hard. They have earned their stripes; now they're costing us range.

- **"Loud revolutions break things. The boring one [verb]s [Name] back."** — Used in 7 of 9 posts. **Retire for 30 days.** When it returns, use it exactly once a quarter as the signature it was meant to be.
- **"We've been thinking about [Name] a lot lately."** — 8 of 9. The pivot was charming once. It's now the bell at the end of a meditation app. **Retire for 14 days. Vary the bridge.**
- **"The pen is still yours. The X is still yours. The remembering is the part we'd like to take."** — 5 of 9, verbatim. **Retire entirely.** It's the single most quotable line we have and we have ruined it by repetition. Save it for the brand manifesto on the homepage.
- **"What we want from a tool" / "Romy's job, in our heads, is not to..."** — 7 of 9. The disclaimer is now the section. **Drop the section header in at least half of future posts.** Sometimes Romy can just not appear at all. The product is stronger when it shows up half the time and earns the entrance.
- **"It is almost always one of four sentences in disguise."** — Used twice in three days (*No*, *Quiet*). **No more numbered taxonomies for 30 days.** Or if it returns, give it a different number — three, five, seven — anything but four.
- **"A small assignment, with love ♡"** — 8 of 9 closing sections. Mostly fine, but at this cadence it has become the format, not the kindness. **Skip in 2 of every 5 posts.** Some posts should just *end.*
- **"Walk to the post office on Friday. Drop them in the blue box."** — 2x verbatim, 3x paraphrased. **Done. Never again.**
- **"The future of fundraising does not look like a [smarter X / flashier Y]. It looks like a [development director who...]."** — 6 of 9. The negation→affirmation rhythm has become the only rhythm. **Vary the cadence.** Sometimes assert without negating first.
- **The Marilynne Robinson supporting cast.** Edna, Eileen, Margaret, Helen, Frank, Tomás, Marcus. All over 65 or under 8. **Write a post next week where the donor is 41, divorced, made her money in software, gives in DAF, and has zero patience for a handwritten card.** She exists. We are pretending she doesn't.

## Bottom line

The week's nine posts would, on their own, win any nonprofit-marketing award in 2026. Held against each other, they reveal a writer who has found a chord progression and is playing the same song nine times. The chords are good. The song is good. But Red Bull doesn't release the same song nine times in a row, even if the song is great.

Next week: pick at least one fight that doesn't use any of the four retired phrases. Write at least one post where the protagonist is the buyer, not the donor. And let one post not mention Romy at all — the brand is strong enough to skip a meeting.

The bar is moving. Move with it.
