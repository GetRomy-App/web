## 2. Patterns across the week — last week's template warning was ignored

Last week we flagged eight repeated structural beats and a phrase-by-phrase retire list. **Six of the eight beats appear again this week.** Some got worse. New tics arrived.

**Verbatim recurrences this week:**

- **"Nobody decides to ___. The whole apparatus is built to ___."** — Four posts in seven days:
  - 06-02: *"Nobody decides to underweight the tour. The whole apparatus is built to underweight it for you."*
  - 06-03: *"Nobody decided to underuse the board. The whole apparatus is built to underuse it."*
  - 06-05: *"Nobody on staff is doing anything wrong. The whole apparatus is built to miss this."*
  - 06-06: *"Nobody decides to stop calling donors. The whole apparatus is built to stop you."*
  This is now a chant. Retire until at least 07-01.

- **"What a tool can do is…" / "Where a tool can quietly help."** — 7/7 posts. The product paragraph is in a fixed slot (third or fourth section from the bottom), opens with *"Rōmy doesn't [X]. A person does [Y]"* (5/7), and ends with the public-record source-link line. Six identical product placements last week became seven this week. **The audit asked for one in four; we got seven in seven.**

- **"A small assignment, with love ♡"** closing header — 4/7 (Tour, About Page, Voicemail; the heart shows up on three of those four). The heart glyph was already flagged. It now appears in this audit's predicted lineage as a tell — anyone reading the blog backward will be able to date posts by whether the heart is on or off.

- **"Quietly" / "very privately" / "gently"** — 20 uses of *quietly* alone across 7 posts (vs. ~32 across 6 last week — frequency dropped but density per post is the same). Adverb tics:
  - 06-01: *"with quiet algorithmic confidence"* twice, *"quietly"* once
  - 06-05: *"very privately"* twice, *"quietly"* five times
  - 06-06: *"quietly"* three times, *"gently"* twice
  These are the writer's autopilot. They no longer signal restraint; they signal the same hand.

- **Time-stamped opener at an unusual minute** — 7/7. (9:53, 5:42, kitchen-on-a-Saturday-morning, 11:47, 11:14, "right now," and "this Saturday.") Five of the seven open with a *specific named character + odd-numbered minute*. When every post lands inside the same first-line silhouette, the blog reads as one essay broken into chapters.

- **The 71-year-old former hospital CFO widow on an iPad** is now a recurring character archetype. **Eleanor** in 06-02 ("seventy-one years old," "hospital CFO for thirty-one years," widow) and **Margaret** in 06-05 ("the seventy-one-year-old former hospital CFO," "iPad on the duvet") are *the same person.* Last week's audit warned about character-name leakage; this week we leaked the entire **biography**. A reader who reads Tour on Tuesday and About Page on Friday will (correctly) ask: is this the same donor? Spoiler: yes.

- **"Where a tool can quietly help" as a section header** — verbatim in 06-04, 06-05; near-verbatim variants in 06-02, 06-06, 06-07. Five out of seven.

- **The "boring revolution, again" callback** — appears verbatim in 06-02. Was already a flagged catechism. Continues.

**New tic that crept in this week:** the *"with love"* closer is now a literal salutation in the body — *"A small assignment, with love ♡"* — and three posts now use the same imperative-with-name-as-eternal-presence closing line:

- 06-02: *"Eleanor is on the calendar. She is always on the calendar."*
- 06-05: *"Margaret is on the iPad. She is always on the iPad."*
- 06-06: *"Frances is in the parking lot of the grocery store. Frances is always in the parking lot of the grocery store."*

This was a powerful device the first time. By the third time in five days, it stops sounding like a benediction and starts sounding like a Mad Libs slot.

**Are we drifting toward feature-listing?** No. The product is still humbly framed. We are drifting toward a different failure mode: **incantation.** The blog used to argue. It now chants.

## 3. The week's best post — and why

**The Tour Was the Interview (06-02).**

It worked because it has the three Red Bull ingredients fully load-bearing:

1. **Identity.** It tells the reader who she is. *"You inherited a profession that thinks of the tour as operations and the meeting as development, when in fact the meeting is theater and the tour is the truth."* That sentence draws a line in the sand the reader can stand on one side of. A Red Bull ad doesn't sell taurine — it tells the buyer she is the kind of person who jumps out of a balloon at the edge of space. This post tells Ben he is the kind of development director who can finally name what the wilted ficus has been doing to his pipeline.

2. **Adrenaline.** *"By 10:11 a.m., when Ben finally came down — nine minutes late, no apology, holding two cups of coffee in mugs that didn't match — Eleanor had already decided. She would be polite for the next forty-three minutes. She would take the tour. She would thank Ben warmly at the curb. She would never write the check."* That is a horror scene. The reader's pulse goes up. This is the only post of the week that creates *stakes inside the first three paragraphs.*

3. **Worldview.** "The tour is culture transfer, not information transfer" is a fully built point of view that survives outside the post. You can use it in a board meeting, on a panel, in a sales call. **Mirror this structurally:** specific named character + dread + worldview pivot inside paragraph three. Don't mirror its template scaffolding — that's the part the writer is already copying too well.

## 4. The week's worst post — and what it should have been

**You Don't Have Donors. You Have Neighbors. (06-07).**

Not because the thesis is wrong. The thesis is the best one of the week. The post fails because it is the only one with **no person in it.** Mrs. Whitcomb is named once. The board chair's grandkids play soccer in the third paragraph and never appear again. The casserole gets two sentences. The whole essay is vocabulary surgery on an empty operating table — and we already learned, on 06-02 and 06-04, that this writer is best when there is a specific seventy-one-year-old woman doing one specific small thing on a specific day. "Neighbors" without a neighbor is "Mary" without Mary.

It should have been: *one specific moment of neighborliness costing nothing and outperforming a quarter of cultivation work.* The casserole graf was the actual post. It got buried in section four.

**Suggested rewritten hook (2–3 lines):**

> Pat brought a casserole to Mrs. Reeves on the Thursday after the diagnosis. Tuna noodle, in a Pyrex dish she did not expect back. She is the executive director of the food bank. She was not, in that moment, doing development.
>
> Mrs. Reeves changed her will the following spring. The food bank was not in it — and then it was.
>
> You don't have donors. You have neighbors. And the dish is still in her cabinet.

Same thesis. One person. One dish. One will. The reader feels what *neighbor* means before the post tells her.

## 5. Three angles to try next week — each a stance, not a topic

Last week we offered three angles; two of them shipped (Capacity Scores 06-01, Most Boards 06-03). The third — *"The gala is the worst place to meet a donor"* — is still on the table. Three new stances for the writer to argue with:

1. **"The development director is a grief counselor with a fundraising quota."** Defendable: most major gifts are made on the back of a loss the development office is structurally unequipped to name. Bequests are children that died first. Tribute gifts are unfinished conversations. We trained development officers in sales theory and sent them to do hospice work. This is the post that finally explains why Eleanor (06-02) and Frances (06-06) and Mary (06-04) all happen to be widows. Name the pattern instead of repeating it.

2. **"Your CRM is a haunted spreadsheet."** Defendable: every name in the file is a person whose context died with the staffer who knew them. We don't have a database. We have a graveyard, and we keep importing more bodies. Snaps the "tea on the kitchen counter" mood. Brings teeth. Could be the first post in three weeks that doesn't open with a named character at a specific minute — and that would be a feature, not a bug.

3. **"The next major gift in America is being made in a group text the development office can't see."** Defendable: the gift is committee work happening between the donor, her adult daughter, her financial advisor, and the cousin in Phoenix. The named donor on the gift line is the last person to sign off, not the first person to decide. The development office is courting one person; the actual decision is being made by four. (Bonus: forces a Field Notes piece about the adult-child gatekeeper, who has not appeared in any post yet and is overdue.)

Bar: each of these will make a working fundraiser *argue back* on the headline. If a fundraiser nods at the title, we lost.

## 6. Brand voice drift watch — extended retire list

The drift is no longer subtle. We are repeating verbatim across weeks. The following needs to go on the bench for at least two weeks. Some of these were on last week's retire list and **shipped anyway.**

**Already flagged last week, used again this week — escalate to hard ban:**

- *"with love ♡"* closing + heart glyph — used 3+ times this week despite being flagged. The heart is now a date stamp.
- *"a person does that — has to … that part stays yours. It always was."* (and its variants) — 5/7 this week. The jingle continues.
- *"every claim linked to where it came from"* / *"from the public record"* — 6/7 this week. Product paragraph boilerplate.
- *"the boring revolution, again"* — 06-02. Still alive after we asked for it to be retired or branded.

**New retires for this week:**

- **"Nobody decides to ___. The whole apparatus is built to ___."** — 4/7. Verbatim construction. Bench until 07-01.
- **The "[Name] is on the [object]. [Name] is always on the [object]."** closing — 3/7. It was a benediction the first time and a meme by the third.
- **"Quietly" / "very privately" / "gently"** — the writer's hand is now visible. Cut the count by half next week. Track it.
- **The 71-year-old former hospital CFO widow archetype** — Eleanor and Margaret are the same person. Vary the donor age, profession, gender, and class. Try a fifty-eight-year-old electrician's widow. Try a forty-one-year-old single mother who just inherited. Try a seventy-three-year-old man whose son made the gift in his honor while he was still alive.
- **The time-stamp opener with odd-numbered minute** — 7/7. Try opening a post with a piece of paper. With a sentence the donor said. With a number. With a question. Anything other than *"At 9:53 a.m., [Name] arrived…"*
- **The Tuesday/Wednesday small-assignment CTA** — 4/7. The reader can predict the closing by the second paragraph. Vary the close: end on a question, end on a fact, end on a one-line scene, end without a CTA at all.
- **"Where a tool can quietly help" as a section header** — 5/7. This phrase has become the product break. Either drop the header entirely and let the worldview do the selling, or **stop putting the product graf in the same slot in the post.**

### Structural recommendations

1. **Skip a day this week.** Last week the writer shipped 6/7 and we said the missing day was an opportunity. This week the writer shipped 7/7 and the template got worse. The daily cadence is what is creating the liturgy. Try a 5-of-7 week with two of those posts deliberately violating the formula.

2. **One post next week with no product paragraph.** Zero. No "where a tool can help." Let the worldview carry. If the post needs the product graf to feel like a Rōmy post, it wasn't a Rōmy-shaped argument to begin with.

3. **Maintain a character names file.** Eleanor / Margaret / Mary / Frances / Helen / Diane / Priya / Karen / Sasha / Ben — at the current cadence we are inventing two named humans per post. We have leaked a character archetype across two posts in five days. Track the file. The audience reads us serially; the names need to behave serially.

4. **The product paragraph rewrite.** *"Rōmy doesn't write the card / water the ficus / make the call. A person does."* is the same paragraph in five posts. Rotate four different product framings and don't repeat one inside a calendar week.
