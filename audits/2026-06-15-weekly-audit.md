## 3. The week's best post — and why

**Your CRM Is a Haunted Spreadsheet (06-13).**

It works because it does what the Tour did last week without using the Tour's machinery. The three Red Bull ingredients are all here, but they arrive through new shapes:

1. **Identity.** *"You inherited a haunted spreadsheet. That is not your fault."* The reader is told who she is in the second-person plural of the development sector — and the sentence absolves her of the shame she has been carrying about her own CRM for years. *Permission to feel betrayed by your software* is a powerful identity gift. The reader walks away believing she is the kind of development director who can name what was done to her file. That is a worldview, not a tactic.

2. **Adrenaline.** Diane's last day — *"a card, a cake, and a Zoom toast"* — and the realization that *"the seven years she did not give to anyone"* in Mrs. Harlow's file is the seven years between two deaths in her family. The pulse goes up not because something is happening fast but because something irreversible *has already happened* and the post is naming it. The mid-post arithmetic — *"by year ten, your file contains roughly one and a half percent of what your institution actually knew about each donor in 2013"* — is the dread.

3. **Worldview.** *"We don't have a database. We have a graveyard."* A fully-built point of view that survives outside the post. You can use it in a board meeting. On a panel. In a sales call. On a podcast. **Mirror this structurally:**
   - **Cold open on an object, not a person.** Open on the file. Open on the brass plate. Open on the recognition wall. Open on the proposal in the printer tray. Open on the spreadsheet. Anything but Eleanor in the lobby at 9:53.
   - **One brave meta-paragraph per post that names what Rōmy is.** The 06-13 parenthetical is the model — direct, slightly contrite, no marketing voice. *"We will not pretend otherwise."* Run that beat in one post a week.
   - **Imperative close with no apology and no heart.** *"Don't make him start the file over again from row one."* The reader closes the tab and remembers the sentence.

## 4. The week's worst post — and what it should have been

**The Gift Was Decided in the Car (06-08).**

Not because the prose is weak — line for line it is some of the most polished writing on the blog. The forest-green Subaru, the salmon, *"did Maya get the part,"* the cork half-out at 6:40 — every object is staged like a Mike Mills movie. The post fails on a higher altitude:

1. **It is the third version of the same thesis in two weeks.** The lobby (06-02), the bedroom (06-05), now the kitchen island. *"The gift is decided in a room you are not in"* — we have made this point three times. A reader who reads us serially closes 06-08 thinking, *I know this argument. I knew it on Tuesday.*

2. **Eleanor is the same archetype the audit asked the writer to retire.** Seventy-something, widow-coded, careful, Subaru. The character is well-drawn but she is the same character drawn last week. Worse, she is named *Eleanor* — the same name as last week's hospital-CFO widow. The reader who remembers Eleanor from 06-02 will assume continuity. There is none. We are running a TV show whose recurring lead is being recast every episode, and nobody is telling the audience.

3. **It opened a three-day chant.** The closing line — *"She is at the kitchen island at 6:40. She is always at the kitchen island at 6:40"* — kicked off a three-post benediction sequence with the same construction across 06-08, 06-09, 06-10. This post is patient zero for the worst voice tic of the week.

It should have been: **a post that doesn't reuse last month's room.** The actual stance worth defending — that the *script* the donor speaks at home four hours later is the deliverable, not the proposal — is sharp. The post should have been built around the *writing* of that script, not around another scene of a donor in a car. Less staging. More craft.

**Suggested rewritten hook (2–3 lines):**

> Your job is to write a three-sentence script for someone who does not know she is going to perform it.
>
> The donor will read your script aloud at 6:40 tonight, at her own kitchen island, to her husband, over the cork of a bottle of red. She will think she is improvising. She isn't. You wrote the lines on Wednesday afternoon, in the briefing you handed her at lunch — and she has been quietly memorizing them ever since.
>
> She will not credit you. She is not supposed to. The script is the gift you give before the gift she gives back.

Same argument. No Subaru. No Eleanor. No 2:14. The reader feels the *writing* of the script before the post tells her the script existed.

## 5. Three angles to try next week — each a stance, not a topic

Last week's audit offered three angles. All three shipped this week — and the three were the three best posts of the week (06-11 Group Text, 06-12 Hospice Work, 06-13 Haunted Spreadsheet). This is now a working system: stances proposed in the audit land as exemplary posts five to nine days later. Three new stances for the writer to argue with:

1. **"The mid-level donor is the major-gifts pipeline. The major-gifts pipeline is a vanity project."** Defendable: the working math of American philanthropy says the $1,500-a-year faithful donor of nineteen years is worth more, on present value, than the prospect the wealth screen flagged. The major-gifts officer is paid to chase the latter and ignore the former. The screen is the problem, but the *org chart* is the deeper problem. This is the angry post the blog has not yet written. It would also give us a non-grief, non-widow protagonist — say, a development associate at a midsize land trust who quietly outperforms the major-gifts officer two cubicles over.

2. **"The naming opportunity is a 1990s technology."** Defendable: rooms named for living people are an asset class built on the assumption that the donor wants visible permanence. The donors writing real money in 2026 want the *opposite* — anonymity, lateness in the giving, the bench by the lake with the brass plate that nobody walks past. The capital campaign deck still leads with the $5M auditorium and the $250k seminar room. Nobody under 65 wants either. Force the writer to argue this in front of a hypothetical board chair. The post writes itself.

3. **"The adult child is the next development director."** Defendable: the Cora character from 06-11 was a glimpse. She is the future donor *and* the present gatekeeper. We have never staged her in her own kitchen, on her own laptop, deciding which of her mother's ten orgs survive the next inheritance. This is a *generational* post the blog has not yet written. Pair it with a specific fact: the largest transfer of wealth in human history is in motion right now, and the development office is still talking to the 70-year-old in the recliner and not the 41-year-old on the iPhone. **This forces the writer to put a 41-year-old at the center of a post for the first time all month.**

Bar, as last week: each headline should make a working fundraiser *argue back.* If a fundraiser nods at the title, we picked the wrong stance.
